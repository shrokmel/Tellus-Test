Stages 1 and 2 are unchanged. Frame, time and units as before: ecliptic-like frame with pole N, t in days since observations began (last observation day 36525), AU and days, angles in degrees.

## 1. Equations and constants used for N

**Law 1 (Tellus, Kepler orbit about the Sun), unchanged from stage 1.**
Tellus heliocentric position: nu_E from Kepler's equation with M_S = 2 pi t / P_E + M0_S; R = (1 - e_E^2)/(1 + e_E cos nu_E); lambda_E = varpi_S + nu_E + pi; position = R (cos lambda_E, sin lambda_E, 0).
- P_E = 365.257550 d (uncertainty not formally estimated)
- e_E = 0.019980
- varpi_S = 3.9069 deg
- M0_S = 152.2598 deg

**Law 2 (Kepler's third law, Newtonian gravity about S), unchanged.** a_N = (P_N / P_E)^(2/3) AU.

**Law 11 (body N, heliocentric Kepler orbit about S), new for N.** With mean anomaly M = 2 pi t / P_N + M0, E from M = E - e sin E, nu from E, r = a (1 - e cos E), u = omega + nu:
x = cos Omega cos u - sin Omega sin u cos i; y = sin Omega cos u + cos Omega sin u cos i; z = sin u sin i; heliocentric position = r (x, y, z).
Geocentric direction = normalised (position_N - position_Tellus), converted to the data frame by v = x EX + y EY + z N.

Constants (best member of the fitted family, `predict_3.py`):
- P_N = 97998 d, uncertainty roughly 45000-98000 d (the family is degenerate in period; see section 4)
- e_N = 0.9428 +/- 0.01
- i_N = 52.880 +/- 0.03 deg
- Omega_N = 105.144 +/- 0.02 deg
- omega_N = 70.469 +/- 0.02 deg
- M0_N = 226.36 +/- 15 deg (at t = 0; the phase is tied to P_N, so its error grows with the period error)
- Corresponding a_N = (P_N/P_E)^(2/3) = 41.6 AU; perihelion q = a(1 - e) = 2.38 AU; aphelion Q = a(1 + e) = 80.8 AU.

Why these equations: the N arc is well described by a Kepler orbit about S seen from Tellus, with the orbital plane (i, Omega, omega) very well constrained by the 207 sightings. The period and phase are not. No revision of stage 1 or 2 laws is needed for N; no Tellus-perturbation term was used because N's closest approach to Tellus within the forecast is about 3.1 AU, far outside the close-approach regime that failed for the faint bodies in stage 2.

## 2. Predictions

`predictions_3.csv` (1461 rows: day 36530 to 43830, every 5 days, 20 years after the last observation) was generated by `predict_3.py`, which implements Laws 1, 2 and 11 with the constants above.

Check against the observed arc (days 36233-36525, not an out-of-sample test): median angular error 1.1 deg, maximum 1.3 deg. Sample: day 36233 observed lon 52.25 lat 1.67, predicted 52.95 / 0.91; day 36525 observed 116.56 / 21.73, predicted 116.38 / 20.53. So the best fit does not reach the 60" noise floor; the residual is about 1 deg, which is the model's own error on the arc.

## 3. N's path over the next 20 years

- Geocentric longitude advances from 118.8 (day 36530) to 175.7 at 1 year, 214.9 at 5 years, 226.2 at 10 years and 235.4 at 20 years. Longitude speed is fastest early (about 57 deg/yr in year 1) and slows to a couple of degrees per year by year 20.
- Geocentric latitude rises to a peak of 22.5 deg about 0.6 yr after the last observation (day 36745), then falls steadily: 7.8 deg at 1 yr, -10.6 at 5 yr, -15.9 at 10 yr, -19.9 at 20 yr (the 20-year end point is also the minimum of the forecast).
- Heliocentric distance is 2.84 AU on day 36525 and rises to 35.2 AU by day 43830, so N has just passed perihelion (q = 2.38 AU) and is receding; it reaches only about 35 of its 81 AU aphelion within the forecast. Distance from Tellus stays between about 3.1 and 35.5 AU over the forecast, so no close approach to Tellus is predicted.
- Interpretation: N is a long-period body on a steeply inclined (about 53 deg) highly eccentric orbit; on the sky it swings through the ecliptic's northern side and crosses to the south over the next 20 years.

## 4. Confidence and expected error

Errors are estimated from the spread of the family of acceptable orbits (members within 1.5x the best cost; P varying from about 45000 to 98000 d, with the other elements nearly fixed), added to the 1.1 deg arc residual:
- After 1 year: about 1.5 deg (family spread about 0.9 deg median, up to 1.6 deg).
- After 5 years: about 3 deg (family spread about 2.5 deg median, up to 4.3 deg).
- After 20 years: about 6-9 deg (family spread about 4.9 deg median, up to 9 deg).

Confidence: the inclination, node and argument of perihelion (the plane and orientation of the orbit): about 75%. The period and phase, and so the 5- and 20-year predictions: about 35%. The model form itself (Kepler about S with no perturbation): about 60% over the 20-year window, given that the arc fits to 1.1 deg and no close approach to Tellus is forecast.

What would prove me wrong: (a) the year-101 sightings disagreeing with the 1-year prediction (lon 175.7, lat 7.8) by more than about 3 deg; (b) the latitude peak not appearing near day 36745 (about 0.6 yr after the last observation) at about 22.5 deg; (c) a sudden change in N's motion that is not a Tellus close approach (the forecast has none), which would indicate an unmodelled perturber; (d) the period turning out well outside 45000-98000 d, which would move the 20-year path further than the family spread suggests.
