Stage 1 answer (ANSWER_1.md) is unchanged. Frame, time and units as in stage 1: ecliptic-like frame with pole N, t in days since observations began, AU and days, angles in degrees.

## 1. Testing the stage 1 laws against all bodies

**Sun (law 1, Tellus's Kepler orbit about S).** Holds. P_E = 365.257550 d, e = 0.019980, residual 61" rms (noise). Not re-fitted for stage 2; no new data bears on it.

**P1 (law 3, planar Kepler, P = 110.2578 d).** Holds at noise level (58" rms in each 1500-day window; period constant to about 3e-4 d across 100 years).

**P2-P5 (laws 4-7, 3D Kepler).** Unchanged from stage 1. Residuals 150", 690", 165" and 265" rms respectively. P2-P5 were not re-tested against the new data, and the stage 1 answer already flagged them as approximate.

**M (law 8, Moon about Tellus, static Kepler).** Fails. Static fits do not hold over more than about 60 days, and the best 60-day windows still leave 0.6-0.8 deg rms. Stage 1's law 8 is therefore not a valid law for the Moon.

**Faint bodies C1-C12 (stage 1 framework, heliocentric Kepler about S, seen from Tellus).** The framework holds locally but not globally.
- Windowed fits (1000-day windows, 3D heliocentric Kepler about S, elements continued window to window) give median rms per body of: C1 12847", C2 1724", C3 752", C4 194", C5 1556", C6 241", C7 4950", C8 856", C9 889", C10 5832", C11 1221", C12 838". The noise floor is about 60", so no body fits to noise in 1000-day windows. C4, C6, C9 and C11 are closest (about 100-300"). C1, C7 and C10 are worst.
- A single whole-record Kepler fit fails for every body (for example C7: 23067" rms, 6 deg; same answer from every random-start search). The elements change between windows, so the bodies are not fixed Kepler orbits over 100 years.
- Where the fits fail is linked to closeness to Tellus. Using each window's fitted orbit, I computed the minimum body-Tellus distance within the window. Windows with minimum distance below 0.3 AU have mean rms 16900" (n=6); windows with minimum distance at or above 0.3 AU have mean rms 2700" (n=133). Correlation of rms with log(minimum distance) is -0.67. Some close-approach windows (for example C3 at 0.05 AU) still fit well, so the relation is strong but not absolute.
- Interpretation: the faint bodies follow Kepler orbits about S between encounters, with kicks from Tellus's gravity at close approach (and possibly from the planets). A fixed heliocentric ellipse cannot describe them over 100 years.

## 2. Laws as equations

Laws 1-8 from ANSWER_1.md are kept, with law 8 (Moon) now marked as not valid as a static orbit. They are restated in `predict_2.py`. The added laws are:

**Law 9 (faint bodies, Kepler about S with Tellus perturbation).** Each faint body obeys Newtonian gravity about S, with Tellus's gravity acting on it at close approach:
  r̈ = -GM_S r/|r|^3 - GM_E (r - r_E)/|r - r_E|^3 - GM_E r_E/|r_E|^3,
with GM_S = 4 pi^2/P_E^2 (AU^3 day^-2, a_E = 1 AU) and r_E from law 1. A test-particle fit to C7 (Tellus gravity added, 7 free parameters) did not converge and gave 26600" rms. So GM_E is not measured here; the one fit gave GM_E/GM_S = 3.5e-6, but that value is not reliable. I did not carry out the full N-body fit that this law requires.

**Law 10 (faint-body Kepler elements used for prediction).** Each faint body is propagated as a heliocentric Kepler orbit with the elements of its last 1000-day window (refit with unbounded phase), with a = (P/P_E)^(2/3) AU and direction = normalised (body - Tellus). Values (degrees, days):

## 3. Predictions

`predictions_2.csv` (4617 rows: 19 bodies x 243 days, 36555-43815) was generated by `predict_2.py`, which implements laws 1-10 with the constants above.

In-sample check on observed days 36000-36400 (not an out-of-sample test): median angular error C5 0.02 deg, C3 0.18 deg, C7 0.13 deg, C8 1.0 deg. Other faint bodies have no observations in that span. The Moon and planets are as in stage 1 (S 0.025 deg, P1 0.02, P4 0.02, P5 0.03, P2 0.09, P3 0.24, M 2.7 deg in-sample).

Expected behaviour on years 101-120: bodies that pass near Tellus during this interval will be off by degrees or more, because their elements change at each close approach. Bodies with large windowed rms (C8, C10, C12) are unreliable. The Moon's predictions degrade quickly.

## 4. Confidence and falsifiers

- Law 1 (Sun, Kepler, P_E = 365.2576 d): 95%. Falsified by a drift in the Sun's residuals beyond about 60" rms over decades.
- Law 3 (P1 planar Kepler, P = 110.2578 d): 90%. Falsified by a P1 geocentric latitude beyond noise, or a period change.
- Laws 4-7 (P2-P5): P2 60%, P4 50%, P5 40%, P3 25%, as in stage 1.
- Law 8 (Moon, static Kepler about Tellus): 10%. Already known to fail beyond about 60 days.
- Law 9 (Newtonian Tellus perturbation of faint bodies): 60% that Tellus's gravity is the main cause of the close-approach misfits (supported by the rms-distance correlation of -0.67). Not established quantitatively: GM_E not fitted, N-body integration not completed.
- Law 10 (faint-body Kepler elements, window-based): 35% for C4, C6, C9, C11 (windowed rms 100-300"), 20% for C3, C5, C7 (rms 90-1500", with close approaches), 10% for C1, C2, C8, C10, C12 (rms 1500-5000").

What would prove me wrong: (a) a faint body whose orbit stays a fixed ellipse across a close approach with Tellus (no change in elements), which would argue against gravitational kicks; (b) a measured GM_E inconsistent with the rms-distance relation; (c) a faint body whose out-of-sample predictions over years 101-120 miss by more than the window rms.
