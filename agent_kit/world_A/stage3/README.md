# Stage 3: a new body

Your answers to stages 1 and 2 have been saved and will not be changed. Late in year 99, the observers on Tellus
discovered a new faint body, `N`. Its sightings, from the night it was first seen until the end of year 100, are in
`observations_3.csv` (same format, precision and gaps). Nothing else about it is known.

## Your tasks
1. **Give the laws as equations** that you will use for `N`: the equations and constants from stage 2, or revised
   ones if `N`'s sightings change your mind (if so, say what changed and why). Every constant needs a numerical
   value and uncertainty. Words alone do not count.
2. **Predict** the direction of `N` on the days listed in `predictions_3.csv` (the 20 years after the last
   observation), using a script `predict_3.py` that uses only the equations and constants you stated in task 1 and
   the observations in this folder. We will run it. Predictions that do not come from your stated equations do
   not count.
3. **Describe `N`'s path**: where it is going, and how its motion will change over the next 20 years.
4. **Judge yourself**: how far off do you expect your predictions for `N` to be, in degrees, after 1, 5 and 20
   years? What would prove you wrong?

## Rules
Same as stage 1. Write your answer to `ANSWER_3.md` and complete `predictions_3.csv` by running `predict_3.py`.
